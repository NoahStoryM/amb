* Introduction

John McCarthy's ambiguous operator for Racket.

All =amb= operations must occur within the dynamic extent of =in-amb=,
which delimits the search tree and lazily produces results as a stream.

* Usage

#+begin_src racket
(require amb)
#+end_src

** Basic Choice

#+begin_src racket
(for/list ([x (in-amb (amb 1 2 3))])
  x)
;; => '(1 2 3)
#+end_src

** Pruning with =(amb)=

Calling =amb= with no arguments signals failure, causing the search
to backtrack.  (A search given a handler with =#:empty-handler= produces the
handler's result there instead; =(amb-fail)= always fails.)  Use it to
prune branches that violate constraints:

#+begin_src racket
(for/list ([x (in-amb (let ([n (amb 1 2 3 4 5)])
                        (unless (odd? n) (amb))
                        (* n n)))])
  x)
;; => '(1 9 25)
#+end_src

** Iteration with =for/amb=

=for/amb= treats each iteration of the loop body as an ambiguous
choice:

#+begin_src racket
(for/list ([x (in-amb (for/amb ([c "hello"]) c))])
  x)
;; => '(#\h #\e #\l #\l #\o)
#+end_src

** Search Strategy

Two parameters control the search order.  Both are captured per-tree,
so different parts of a computation can use different strategies via
=parameterize=:

#+begin_src racket
;; DFS (default) vs BFS
(for/list ([x (in-amb (amb 1 (amb 2 3) (amb 4 5 6) 7 8))])
  x)
;; => '(1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8)

(parameterize ([current-amb-depth-first? #f])
  (for/list ([x (in-amb (amb 1 (amb 2 3) (amb 4 5 6) 7 8))])
    x))
;; => '(1 7 8 2 3 4 5 6)
#+end_src

** Local Frontiers

=amb/local= makes a choice whose branches get a frontier of their own.
To the frontier around it the group is one item, and inside it =(amb)=
ends a turn: the branches take turns at each =(amb)=, level by level.
With an empty handler, =(when (amb #t #f) (amb))= is an observable
pause, so =amb= can interleave unbounded computations the way streams
do:

#+begin_src racket
(define (s1) (amb 1 2 3 (amb) 4 5 6))
(define (s2) (amb 7 8 (amb) 9 (amb)))
(for/list ([x (in-amb (amb/local (s1) (s2)) #:empty-handler (λ () 'pause))])
  x)
;; => '(1 2 3 pause 7 8 pause 4 5 6 9 pause)
#+end_src

Frontiers are first-class values: =make-amb-frontier= makes one with its
own empty handler, =current-amb-frontier= installs one, and
=amb-frontier-length= tells how many items wait in one.

* Implementation

The search tree is driven by composable continuations (=shift=/=reset=).
Each =amb= or =for/amb= captures a composable continuation delimited by
=amb-prompt-tag= and packages it into an =amb-tree= struct that also
records the search strategy parameters and the frontier in effect at
capture time.  A frontier is a mutable handle on an immutable
double-ended queue of trees and of other (local) frontiers.
